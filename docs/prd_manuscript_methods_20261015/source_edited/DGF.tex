\begin{abstract}
The rapid decay of a black hole ringdown makes waveform reconstruction sensitive to interruptions near its onset. We present DenoiseGapFiller (DGF), a deep-learning framework for joint denoising and reconstruction of damped oscillatory signals. A dual-branch encoder combines complex time--frequency features with signed multiresolution temporal context; sequence blocks and a waveform decoder synthesize the complete clean signal using the observation and known suppression mask. On a controlled four-component waveform benchmark of 1000 independent test signals, with 33-s suppression intervals in 528-s records, DGF achieves a mean direct noise-weighted mismatch of $5.37\times10^{-3}$, a median of $1.52\times10^{-3}$ and a 95th percentile of $1.01\times10^{-2}$. The mean relative waveform error is 6.50\%, and the mean absolute composite phase error at the signal-envelope peak is $1.50^\circ$. Evaluation uses fixed time and phase and includes the complete test population. We specify the corresponding two-channel LISA formulation with Kerr-mode excitation, a finite-arm time-delay interferometry response, PSD whitening and noise-conditioned waveform synthesis for variable suppression intervals spanning approximately 6\%--10\% of the record. The controlled results establish coherent waveform recovery within the tested signal and noise family and motivate learned reconstruction for ringdown data preprocessing.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The ringdown following a binary black hole merger carries information about the remnant spacetime. In the perturbative regime, its waveform is described by damped quasinormal modes whose frequencies and decay times depend on the mass and spin of a Kerr black hole~\cite{berti2006gravitational,berti2009qnm}. Measuring these modes is the basis of black hole spectroscopy and ringdown tests of general relativity~\cite{Isi_2019}. Space-based observatories such as LISA, Taiji and TianQin offer access to the ringdowns of massive black hole binaries in the millihertz band~\cite{amaroseoane2017laserinterferometerspaceantenna,robson2019construction,Ruan2020,Luo2016}.

Ringdown amplitudes decay rapidly. An interruption near the onset can therefore remove a substantial fraction of the signal even when its duration is short compared with the analysis window. Scheduled interruptions and unscheduled data losses are relevant to space-based observations~\cite{Baghi2019,dey2021gaps}. Shi \textit{et al.}~\cite{shi2024effectsdatagapsringdown} examined their effects on ringdown observability and parameter estimation. Multiplication by a gap window also redistributes power across Fourier frequencies~\cite{Harris1978}. The reconstruction problem consequently concerns the phase and decay envelope across the suppressed interval, as well as the complete waveform. An interval covering 20\% of a short record illustrates a severe interruption; the variable-duration LISA protocol considered here covers approximately 6\%--10\% of a 528-s record.

Existing approaches exploit different information about the missing signal. Interpolation uses neighboring samples, with accuracy determined by the bandwidth, sampling and interpolation rule~\cite{Horowitz1974,Maeland1988,Unser1999}. Bayesian data augmentation treats missing samples as latent variables in source-parameter inference~\cite{Baghi2019}. Sparse inpainting exploits structure in Fourier or wavelet representations and has been applied to LISA galactic-binary signals~\cite{Blelly2021,Starck2010}. Learned dictionaries provide another way to encode waveform structure for denoising~\cite{TorresForne2016}. Ringdown reconstruction poses a specific challenge for these approaches: the waveform is oscillatory, its envelope changes rapidly, and the available context can be strongly asymmetric about the gap.

Neural methods learn reconstruction maps from simulated waveforms and noisy observations. Xu \textit{et al.}~\cite{Xu_2024} used a DenseNet--BiLSTM model for inspiral--merger--ringdown signals under several forms of corruption. Mao \textit{et al.}~\cite{Mao:2025cae} combined a convolutional autoencoder with a bidirectional gated recurrent unit for long LISA sequences containing gaps. WaveFormer~\cite{Wang:2024waveformer} combines convolutional feature extraction and Transformer blocks for denoising ground-based data, and assesses amplitude and phase recovery separately. These studies motivate learned waveform estimation. Their waveform families, detector responses and noise conditions also define the domains in which their reported performance applies.

We develop DGF to recover damped oscillations from noisy observations with a known signal-suppression interval. The time--frequency branch represents local oscillatory content through complex Morlet coefficients. The temporal branch preserves the sign and multiscale structure of the waveform. Mixing layers combine these complementary features, and sequence blocks connect context on both sides of the interval before a decoder synthesizes the clean waveform. Related time-series models use temporal and feature mixing to combine information across scales~\cite{TSMixer2023,TimeMixer2024}. The LISA configuration incorporates PSD whitening, mask-aware and joint-channel modules, and noise-conditioned coefficients in a signal basis fitted to clean training waveforms.

The observation model is $d=Gh+n$: $G$ suppresses the signal over one interval, and noise is present throughout the record. We assess recovery through direct waveform mismatch, amplitude residuals, error spectra and composite analytic phase. Full-population distributions establish the typical accuracy and its upper tail; separate peak-masking statistics resolve the effect of losing the strongest part of the signal. Selected successful reconstructions illustrate the oscillations recovered within informative gaps. The quantitative results reported here use the controlled waveform and noise prescription in Appendix~\ref{app:sequence_modeling}; the physical LISA source and channel conventions are specified in Sec.~\ref{sec:method}.

Section~\ref{sec:method} presents the reconstruction framework, signal and noise models, and training protocol. Section~\ref{sec:results} evaluates waveform recovery, gap-location dependence, amplitude fidelity and phase accuracy. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses the implications for ringdown analysis and the scope of the simulations. The appendixes provide implementation details, the controlled waveform prescription, complementary diagnostics and training comparisons.

\section{Methodology}\label{sec:method}
DGF estimates the complete clean waveform from the observation and known mask. Its shared components are the dual-branch encoder, sequence blocks, waveform decoder and mask-aware residual synthesis. The controlled single-channel study in Sec.~\ref{sec:results} uses the numerical prescription of Appendix~\ref{app:sequence_modeling}. The two-channel configuration described below specifies a physical LISA source population, response and instrument-noise model, together with whitening and joint-channel synthesis. These configurations retain the same representation-based approach to joint denoising and waveform reconstruction.
\subsection{Physical ringdown signals and LISA response}\label{sec:physical_signals}
The source population consists of nonspinning progenitor binaries. The source-frame total mass $M_{\rm tot}^{\rm src}$ is drawn log-uniformly from $10^5$ to $5\times10^6\,M_\odot$, the light-to-heavy mass ratio $q=m_2/m_1$ uniformly from $0.3$ to $1$, and redshift $z$ uniformly from $1$ to $6$. Sky position and inclination are isotropic; polarization and orbital phase are uniform on their angular ranges. We use a flat $\Lambda$CDM distance relation with $H_0=67.4\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ and $\Omega_m=0.315$.

The SEOBNRv4 remnant prescription implemented in LALSimulation determines the source-frame remnant mass $M_f^{\rm src}$ and dimensionless spin $\chi_f$. The Kerr-mode frequencies and damping times use the detector-frame remnant mass,
\begin{equation}
M_f^{\rm det}=(1+z)M_f^{\rm src}.
\end{equation}
Four fundamental modes, $(\ell,m,n)=(2,2,0),(2,1,0),(3,3,0),(4,4,0)$, enter the PyCBC time-domain ringdown generator~\cite{berti2006gravitational,berti2009qnm,PyCBCRingdown}. Spherical angular harmonics and luminosity-distance scaling specify the polarizations. Writing $q_\star=1/q$, the mode excitation coefficients are~\cite{Kamaretsos2012}
\begin{align}
\alpha_{22}&=0.25e^{-q_\star/7.5},\nonumber\\
\frac{\alpha_{21}}{\alpha_{22}}&=0.13(q_\star-1)^{1/2},\qquad
\frac{\alpha_{33}}{\alpha_{22}}=0.18(q_\star-1)^{1/3},\nonumber\\
\frac{\alpha_{44}}{\alpha_{22}}&=0.024q_\star^{3/4}.
\end{align}
These fits refer to the reference epoch used by Kamaretsos \textit{et al.}, ten remnant-mass time units after the dominant-mode luminosity peak. The relative phase convention is $\phi_{\ell m}=m\phi_{\rm orb}$. It defines a simplified coherent mode family for this reconstruction study. PyCBC's short pre-reference taper supplies a smooth numerical onset; the modeled damped-mode segment retains the stated excitation normalization.

The polarizations are projected onto the constellation links with \texttt{fastlisaresponse}~\cite{Katz2022Response}. Delayed link combinations produce second-generation TDI $X,Y,Z$ observables in an idealized equal-arm constellation. The analyzed channels are
\begin{equation}
A=\frac{Z-X}{\sqrt{2}},\qquad E=\frac{X-2Y+Z}{\sqrt{6}}.\label{eq:aet_convention}
\end{equation}
Both channels are expressed in fractional-frequency units. We generate the response on a padded parent sequence, remove the padding and select a 528-s analysis window. Reconstruction is evaluated on that finite window, including for signals whose damping times exceed its duration.

\subsection{Instrument noise, calibration and whitening}\label{noise model}
Independent stationary Gaussian $A/E$ instrument-noise realizations are generated from the second-generation TDI sensitivities provided by LISA Analysis Tools, with the \texttt{scirdv1} instrument model and no confusion foreground~\cite{LISAAnalysisTools}. For an interior real-FFT bin, the independent real and imaginary noise quadratures have variance $N_p f_s S_c(f_k)/4$, where $N_p$ is the padded parent length and $c\in\{A,E\}$. The inverse transform and padding removal give the simulated time series.

A fixed PSD $S_{c,\rm cal}(f_k)$ is estimated by averaging one-sided periodograms from 512 independent noise-only segments, each containing $N=1056$ samples at $f_s=2\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Estimating the PSD after cropping accounts for finite-window leakage near transfer-function nulls. The same calibration arrays define whitening, the training loss and the comparison metric for every model. The metric weights Fourier bins diagonally; likelihood calculations must also account for correlations introduced by the finite window.

The observation is whitened by the invertible transformation
\begin{equation}
u_c=W_cd_c=\operatorname{irfft}\!\left[
\frac{\operatorname{rfft}(d_c)_k}{\sqrt{f_sS_{c,\rm cal}(f_k)/2}}\right].\label{eq:whitening}
\end{equation}
At DC and Nyquist, positive neighboring-bin values define the whitening factors. Inverse whitening then restores the original fractional-frequency units. These two bins have zero weight in the primary overlap. The first 128 calibration segments determine the expected white-noise RMS, excluding DC and Nyquist, for the coefficient head. All calibration segments are independent of the signal population and test observations.

\subsection{Analysis windows and variable signal-suppression intervals}\label{sec:gaps}
For each channel we use the observation and binary mask
\begin{equation}
d_{c,j}=G_jh_{c,j}+n_{c,j},\qquad m_j=1-G_j,\label{eq:observation}
\end{equation}
where $G_j=0$ inside the suppressed interval and $G_j=1$ elsewhere. The supervised target is the complete clean $A/E$ waveform $h_{c,j}$. Instrument noise remains present inside the interval. Masking is applied to the physical signal before whitening.

Gap widths are sampled uniformly from the 21 even integers $64,66,\ldots,104$, corresponding to 32--52 s or 6.06\%--9.85\% of the record. Changing the width preserves the reference interval center. Fixed widths of 66 and 100 samples provide additional duration checks. The parent response peak sets the event-centered simulation window, with reference gap offsets drawn between $-66$ and $66$ s. A second seeded draw places the reference interval start at local samples 132--263 and determines the crop. The network receives the resulting observation and known mask at inference.

\subsection{Dual-branch DGF encoder and waveform synthesis}\label{sec:architecture}
Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} shows the DGF architecture. The two $A/E$ channels share encoder weights. The time--frequency branch represents a complex Morlet CWT by its modulus and argument. Sixteen scales and a factor-four temporal subsampling give a $2\times16\times264$ array per channel. Mixing along the temporal, feature and scale axes, followed by convolutional embedding and linear projection, produces $8\times32\times64$ features.

A second Morlet CWT uses 16 logarithmically spaced frequencies from $0.002$ to $0.25\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Three convolutional stages with group normalization and GELU map these low-frequency features to an additive correction before the time--frequency branch projection.

The temporal branch subtracts the observed mean, divides by the observed standard deviation and computes a db4 decomposition. Eight reconstructed subbands preserve signed time-domain information. Each is divided into 32 overlapping patches of 64 samples with stride 32, giving the same $8\times32\times64$ feature shape. The relation $1056=64+31\times32$ fixes the input length.

The branch features are added and passed through token, convolutional and sinusoidal position embeddings. A residual Conv2d layer precedes 16 sequence blocks, each containing channel-wise scaled dot-product attention, normalization and a feedforward layer. The decoder maps features to waveform patches, averages overlapping samples, sums the subbands and applies a Conv1d layer. Restoring the observation normalization gives the channel prediction. Appendix~\ref{app:model_analysis} specifies the attention operation and numerical implementation.

\subsection{Joint-channel refinement and noise-conditioned coefficients}\label{sec:coefficients}
A channel-wise mask-aware residual module receives the observation, intermediate prediction, mask and normalized time coordinate. Its six dilated Conv1d blocks have width 24, kernel size 7 and dilations $1,2,4,8,16,32$, with group normalization and GELU. A second width-32 residual module couples the $A$ and $E$ predictions using the same dilation schedule.

We construct a joint basis $B\in\mathbb R^{2N\times128}$ from the 60,000 clean training waveforms in whitened coordinates. After removing DC and Nyquist, each waveform is normalized to give equal weight to each source. The leading eigenvectors of the resulting second-moment matrix form the basis. The basis and coefficient adapter produce the whitened prediction $b$.

The coefficient head predicts the waveform coordinates through
\begin{align}
c_0&=B^{\mathsf T}P_0b,\qquad
c=c_0+0.1s\tanh F_\psi(z),\label{eq:refinement}\\
\widehat u&=Bc+(I-P_0)b,\qquad
\widehat h=W^{-1}\widehat u.\nonumber
\end{align}
The operator $P_0$ removes DC and Nyquist in each channel. The scale $s=\operatorname{RMS}(u)\sqrt{2N}$ uses a small RMS floor for numerical stability. Equation~\eqref{eq:refinement} preserves the endpoint components of $b$. With a zero-initialized final layer, the head reproduces the starting coefficient prediction to floating-point precision.

The 470-dimensional input $z$ contains 262 normalized waveform, coefficient and mask coordinates; 16 absolute-noise and spectral-energy features; and 192 pooled continuous-phase features. The noise features use endpoint-excluded observed RMS, the independently calibrated noise RMS, coarse observed frequency-band energy ratios and RMS residuals between observation and prediction. For the physical-frequency CWT, continuous phase is encoded as
\begin{equation}
a_C\{1,\cos\theta_C,\sin\theta_C\},\quad
a_C=\log(1+|C|),\quad\theta_C=\arg C.\label{eq:continuous_phase}
\end{equation}
Amplitude weighting makes the phase features approach zero as the local CWT amplitude approaches zero. Pooling these features to a $4\times8$ grid in each channel gives 192 entries. The head maps 470 inputs to width 192, applies two residual MLP blocks and outputs 128 coefficients. Each residual block uses layer normalization, two linear layers, GELU and dropout 0.05. The coefficient head has 264,128 parameters. The complete stored model has 9,589,685 parameters.

At inference, the coefficient head uses only the observation, current prediction, known mask and fixed noise calibration. Source parameters and clean targets enter simulation, training supervision and evaluation.

\subsection{Direct mismatch, training objective and source separation}\label{loss}
We define the direct joint-channel inner product by
\begin{equation}
(a|b)_S=4\Delta f\operatorname{Re}
\sum_{c\in\{A,E\}}\sum_{k=1}^{N/2-1}
\frac{\widetilde a_{c,k}\widetilde b_{c,k}^{*}}{S_{c,\rm cal}(f_k)},\label{eq:joint_inner}
\end{equation}
where $\widetilde a=\Delta t\,\operatorname{rfft}(a)$, $\Delta t=1/f_s$ and $\Delta f=f_s/N$. The direct mismatch and full-window optimal signal norm are
\begin{equation}
\mathcal M_{AE}=1-\frac{(h|\widehat h)_S}{\sqrt{(h|h)_S(\widehat h|\widehat h)_S}},\quad
\rho^2=(h|h)_S.\label{eq:joint_mismatch}
\end{equation}
We evaluate the overlap at the original time and phase. For nonzero waveforms, $\mathcal M_{AE}\in[0,2]$, with values above one for anticorrelated predictions. An overall positive rescaling of the PSD cancels in the normalized overlap; its frequency dependence sets the relative bin weights.

The two-channel training objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L=\mathcal M_{AE}+0.2E_S+0.02E_{\rm time,gap},\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{align}
E_S&=\frac{(\widehat h-h|\widehat h-h)_S}{(h|h)_S},\nonumber\\
E_{\rm time,gap}&=\frac{\sum_{c,j}(1+2m_j)(\widehat h_{c,j}-h_{c,j})^2}{\sum_{c,j}h_{c,j}^2}.\label{eq:auxiliary_errors}
\end{align}
The objective combines waveform agreement, a noise-weighted amplitude residual and a time-domain residual with three times the weight inside the gap. Losses and population mismatches are arithmetic means over sources.

Figure~\ref{fig:training_workflow} summarizes the training protocol. The training set contains 60,000 distinct physical sources. Each source contributes two variable-width observations and one fixed 33-s observation with an additional independent noise realization. Selected views undergo a global sign reversal of both observation and target. CWT and db4 features are recomputed for each view. One epoch contains 180,000 observations.

The validation set contains 1,500 independent sources. Model selection uses the mean direct mismatch over this complete set with uniformly sampled variable gap widths. Fixed 33- and 50-s gaps provide additional checks on the same sources. The test protocol reserves 6,000 independent source seeds and generates their observations after model selection. Population summaries include all generated sources; SNR-conditioned summaries state the corresponding sample counts.

\subsection{Training protocol}\label{sec:training}
The LISA configuration uses a two-stage procedure. First, the coefficient head is trained with the encoder and waveform synthesis modules held fixed. Backbone predictions and observation features are cached for this stage. AdamW uses batches of 1024, weight decay $3\times10^{-4}$ and a five-epoch warmup to learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$. The learning rate is halved on a validation plateau. Training has a maximum of 600 epochs, minimum 100, patience 100 and improvement threshold $10^{-6}$.

The second stage jointly trains the decoder, physical-frequency adapter, channel-wise and joint residual modules, coefficient adapter and head. The encoder, transforms and signal basis remain fixed. This stage has 422,151 trainable parameters. Group learning rates range from $5\times10^{-7}$ for the decoder to $10^{-4}$ for the head. It uses batches of 128, the same weight decay, a maximum of 200 epochs, minimum 40 and patience 50. Validation plateaus trigger further learning-rate reductions.

Raw and exponential-moving-average weights, with decay factor 0.995, are evaluated using the complete validation mean. Model selection, early stopping and learning-rate scheduling use validation sources. The independent test set is reserved for the selected estimator. Appendix~\ref{app:model_analysis} gives the numerical conventions, and Appendix~\ref{app:training_checks} describes feature selection and supplementary training comparisons.

\section{Reconstruction results}\label{sec:results}
We evaluate DGF on 1000 independent four-component damped waveforms generated by the controlled prescription in Appendix~\ref{app:sequence_modeling}. Each record contains 1056 samples at 2 Hz and one 66-sample signal-suppression interval. The corresponding durations are 528 s and 33 s. Test seeds 2026240000--2026240999 are disjoint from the residual-module training and validation ranges. All test signals contribute to the population statistics.

The evaluated estimator combines the dual-branch backbone with a mask-aware residual module trained on six noise/mask views per training waveform. It operates in the numerical waveform coordinates defined in Appendix~\ref{app:sequence_modeling}. We report its final waveform predictions throughout this section. Linear interpolation gives a local continuity reference: it fills the suppressed interval using neighboring noisy observations and retains the observation outside that interval. Its error includes the retained noise over the full record. Supplementary comparisons of fitted DGF variants appear in Appendix~\ref{app:training_checks}.

\subsection{Direct waveform mismatch}\label{subsec:Mismatch}
The time-domain normalized overlap and mismatch are
\begin{equation}
\mathcal M_t=1-\frac{\sum_jh_j\widehat h_j}{\sqrt{\sum_jh_j^2\sum_j\widehat h_j^2}}.
\end{equation}
For a noise-weighted comparison we define
\begin{align}
(a|b)_S&=4\Delta f\operatorname{Re}\sum_{k=1}^{T/2-1}\frac{\widetilde a_k\widetilde b_k^*}{S_{\rm cal}(f_k)},\\
\mathcal M_S&=1-\frac{(h|\widehat h)_S}{\sqrt{(h|h)_S(\widehat h|\widehat h)_S}},\label{eq:mismatch}
\end{align}
where $\widetilde a_k=\Delta t\,\mathrm{rFFT}(a)_k$, $T=1056$, $\Delta t=0.5$ s, and $\Delta f=1/(T\Delta t)$. The primary metric uses the rectangular segment and excludes DC and Nyquist. The shared normalization constants cancel in the overlap. Both metrics use the original time and phase conventions. With signed real overlaps, mismatch lies in $[0,2]$ and can exceed one for an anticorrelated reconstruction. A low PSD increases a frequency bin's relative contribution to the normalized inner product. A constant rescaling of the entire PSD leaves Eq.~\eqref{eq:mismatch} unchanged.

\subsection{Gap location and waveform recovery}
The clean-envelope peak falls inside the suppressed interval for 121 test signals. DGF's mean direct noise-weighted mismatch in this subgroup is 0.02771, with median 0.00320 and 95th percentile 0.11283. For the 879 signals whose peaks lie outside the interval, the mean is 0.00229. Suppression of the dominant early cycles therefore produces a larger and more dispersed error than suppression of weaker waveform segments.

\subsection{Amplitude and spectral fidelity}\label{subsec:MeanSquaredError}\label{subsec:AmplitudeDensityFrequencySpectrum}
Normalized overlap is invariant under positive amplitude rescaling. We therefore assess amplitude-sensitive waveform error through $E=\|\widehat h-h\|_2/\|h\|_2$. DGF has mean $E=0.06496$, median 0.04753 and 95th percentile 0.14741. The mean signed norm bias, $\|\widehat h\|_2/\|h\|_2-1$, is $-0.00200$. These quantities describe the reconstruction residual and its overall amplitude normalization alongside the overlap measures.

For the 121 signals with peaks inside the suppressed interval, the local relative error $\|m(\widehat h-h)\|_2/\|mh\|_2$ has mean 0.12349 and median 0.05735. This diagnostic measures recovery within the interval containing the waveform peak. Appendix~\ref{app:complementary} also examines intervals with little target signal, for which normalization by $\|mh\|_2$ amplifies small residuals.

Figure~\ref{fig:psd} compares the mean periodogram of the waveform residual $\widehat h-h$ with that of the simulated noise, averaging all 1000 test signals. The residual spectrum complements the overlap and time-domain errors by resolving the frequency dependence of the reconstruction error. The measurement concerns the summed waveform; recovery of individual mode frequencies and damping times calls for component-resolved fitting.

\subsection{Phase recovery}\label{subsec:PhaseRecoveryAlignment}
Let $z=h+i\mathcal H[h]$ and $\widehat z=\widehat h+i\mathcal H[\widehat h]$. At the target-envelope peak $j_\star$, we define the wrapped phase diagnostic
\begin{equation}\Delta\phi_\star=\arg[\widehat z_{j_\star}z_{j_\star}^*],\qquad |\Delta\phi_\star|\in[0,\pi].\end{equation}
We evaluate the composite analytic phase at the target-envelope peak using the original time and phase convention. DGF's mean absolute deviation is $1.50^\circ$. Its median is $0.73^\circ$, 90th percentile $2.69^\circ$ and 95th percentile $4.08^\circ$. Figure~\ref{fig:phase} shows the full distribution and the waveform from Fig.~\ref{fig:examples}(b), whose peak-phase error is $0.096^\circ$. Appendix~\ref{app:complementary} gives the peak-masking subgroup statistics and the dependence on the numerical waveform-to-noise norm ratio.

\section{Discussion and Conclusions}\label{sec:discussion}
We have presented DGF for joint denoising and reconstruction of damped ringdown-like waveforms with signal-suppression intervals. Its dual-branch representation combines local complex time--frequency structure with signed multiresolution waveform context. The decoder and mask-aware synthesis module recover a complete waveform from the corrupted observation. In the controlled independent test, DGF achieves a mean direct noise-weighted mismatch of 0.00537, mean relative waveform error of 0.06496 and mean absolute peak-phase error of $1.50^\circ$. The complete distributions quantify performance beyond the selected illustrations.

Gap location is an important determinant of reconstruction accuracy. The successful peak-masking examples recover missing intervals containing approximately 29\%--30\% of the signal squared norm. Across the complete peak-masking subgroup, the higher mean mismatch and broad upper tail reflect the loss of the dominant early cycles. Reporting the full-record overlap together with amplitude-sensitive and gap-local errors provides a more informative assessment of this problem than any one diagnostic alone.

The numerical measurements use the four-component waveform family and empirical noise construction of Appendix~\ref{app:sequence_modeling}. The physical LISA configuration specifies remnant and detector-frame mass conventions, Kerr-mode excitation, a finite-arm TDI response and fractional-frequency instrument noise. It couples PSD whitening and joint-channel synthesis to noise-conditioned signal-basis coefficients. Its independent assessment uses the source separation and variable-gap protocol in Sec.~\ref{sec:method}. The corresponding simulations cover nonspinning progenitors, an idealized equal-arm constellation and single suppression intervals of approximately 6\%--10\% in 528-s records.

The recovered waveform provides a point estimate of the clean signal conditioned on the observation. It can support waveform inspection and supply initial estimates or proposals for ringdown analysis. Parameter inference uses the original observation likelihood, formed from $d-Gh(\theta)$ and the noise covariance. Posterior bias and coverage tests would quantify the utility of reconstructed waveforms in that setting. Bayesian gap treatment~\cite{Baghi2019} and learned denoising~\cite{Wang:2024waveformer} address complementary stages of this analysis.

Further simulations can extend the reconstruction domain to moving unequal-arm constellations, nonstationary noise, confusion foregrounds, overlapping sources and multiple intervals. A probabilistic reconstruction model would characterize uncertainty in the missing waveform, and repeated training runs would assess variation between fitted estimators. Component-resolved frequency and damping-time measurements would connect waveform fidelity to the requirements of black hole spectroscopy. The present results establish reconstruction accuracy for the tested damped-wave family and provide a defined framework for these physical and statistical assessments.

\section{Implementation and reproducibility}\label{app:model_analysis}
\subsection{Representations and attention}
For each $A/E$ channel, the backbone CWT array has shape $2\times16\times264$, and the temporal patches and fused features have shape $8\times32\times64$. For a feature matrix $Z\in\mathbb R^{32\times64}$, attention is computed as
\begin{equation}
A(Z)=\operatorname{softmax}(ZZ^{\mathsf T}/\sqrt{64})Z.
\end{equation}
Each of the eight feature channels applies this operation, followed by feedforward transformations, residual connections and normalization. The implementation also allocates multihead-attention parameters that are inactive in the forward pass. The quoted total parameter count includes those stored tensors. Attention spans the full sequence.

The db4 representation uses the approximation and seven detail components in library coefficient order. The decoder combines reconstructed patches by overlap averaging and subband summation. Its linear patch map and the encoder embedding map have independent checkpoint values. When loading, we clone the initially transposed storage before reading the tensors so that their saved values are preserved on both CPU and GPU.

\subsection{Numerical and test conventions}
The white-space RMS floor is $10^{-4}$ for coefficient and residual normalization. Training spectral target and prediction squared norms are floored at $10^{-12}$ for finite numerical division, and physical-time relative-error denominators use the same floor in the scaled working representation. Physical evaluation uses float64 transforms, the common frozen PSD and the complete target norm. Overlap is clipped to $[-1,1]$ to bound roundoff. A zero prediction is assigned mismatch one; a zero target triggers a dataset validation failure.

The $A/E$ test seeds are 2038200000--2038205999. The additional fixed-gap training-noise seeds are 2158200000--2158259999. Features are recomputed from each generated observation. The reproducibility record contains raw and EMA checkpoints, training histories, noise calibration, masks, seeds and per-source predictions. SHA-256 manifests identify the source files and fixed assets. Restarting resumes the last completed epoch with its optimizer, scheduler and random-number states.

The simulated response peak determines the event-centered crop and gap metadata. Inference uses the cropped observation, known mask and fixed preprocessing arrays. Clean targets are used in the losses and evaluation. The implementation is available at \url{https://github.com/ljq2088/RingdownGapFilling}; configurations and checkpoint identifiers are included with the evaluation record.

\section{Controlled waveform and noise prescription}\label{app:sequence_modeling}
This appendix defines the signal generator, noise and training procedure used for Sec.~\ref{sec:results}. The physical LISA $A/E$ model is specified in Sec.~\ref{sec:physical_signals}.
\subsection{Signal prescription}
The benchmark contains four oscillatory components labelled $(2,2)$, $(2,1)$, $(3,3)$ and $(4,4)$. Independent coordinates $M\in[10^5,10^6]$, $q\in[0.5,0.8]$ and $z\in[4,6]$ are drawn uniformly, with fixed orientation and phase. The coordinate $M$ enters through $MT_\odot$, while $z$ sets the distance scale. These coordinates define the numerical waveform family; physical remnant masses and redshifted mode frequencies are treated separately in Sec.~\ref{sec:physical_signals}.

Writing $\eta=q/(1+q)^2$ and $\chi=\eta(2\sqrt{3}-3.5171\eta+2.5763\eta^2)$, the implemented frequencies and decay times are
\begin{align}
\omega_a^{\rm b}&=\frac{f_{1a}+f_{2a}(b_a-\chi)^{f_{3a}}}{M T_\odot},\label{eq:benchmark_frequency}\\
\tau_a^{\rm b}&=\frac{2[q_{1a}+q_{2a}(1-\chi)^{q_{3a}}]}{\omega_a^{\rm b}},
\end{align}
Here $b_a=(1,1,3,4)$ in the component order above. Table~\ref{tab:frequency_coefficients} lists the coefficients. The standard Kerr fits have $b_a=1$ for every mode~\cite{berti2006gravitational}. The last two benchmark components therefore follow the frequencies defined by Eq.~\eqref{eq:benchmark_frequency}.

The implemented component multipliers are
\begin{align}

A_{33}^{\rm b}&=0.671+0.157\eta,& A_{44}^{\rm b}&=0.122-0.188\eta-0.964\eta^2.
\end{align}
These multipliers specify the numerical component amplitudes. The physical excitation model in Sec.~\ref{sec:physical_signals} uses the relative-amplitude fits of Ref.~\cite{Kamaretsos2012}. Fixed angular factors and a TianQin-inspired modulation are applied in frequency space before the discrete inverse transform, cyclic shift, crop and amplitude scaling described below.

\subsection{Signal discretization}
The frequency-domain function \texttt{sf} is sampled on \texttt{arange}$(10^{-5},1,10^{-5})$ Hz with $K=99999$ entries. Its response-modulated component sum is passed to \texttt{irfft} with $N=2(K-1)=199996$ and the library normalization $1/N$. This places the first entry at the DC index. The implementation uses the array values directly, with no additional continuous-Fourier sampling-rate factor. After a cyclic shift of $N/2$ samples, 1056 samples are cropped from $(N-1056)/2+s$, where $s$ is a uniform integer in $[0,527]$. The crop is cast to float32 and multiplied by 0.03. These operations define the benchmark amplitude normalization.

The fixed angles are $\iota=\pi/4$, $\gamma=\pi/3$, $\theta_d=1.17662$, $\psi_d=-0.873302$ and $\phi_d=-0.615727$ radians. The TianQin-inspired modulation frequency is approximately $1.99\times10^{-5}$ Hz. These angular and modulation choices define the benchmark response.

\subsection{Noise discretization and evaluation weighting}
Let $u_j$ be independent standard Gaussian variates on the parent sequence and let $S_{\rm ref}$ denote the Welch spectrum of the supplied channel combination. The implementation first interpolates this PSD onto the generator grid, then interpolates its square root onto the parent real-FFT grid. Denoting the resulting array by $B_k$, the synthesized sequence is
\begin{equation}n_j^{\rm parent}=\sqrt{f_s}\,\operatorname{irfft}\left[\frac{\operatorname{rfft}(u)_k}{\sqrt N}B_k\right]_j,\qquad f_s=2\,\mathrm{Hz}.
\end{equation}
It is cropped centrally to 1056 samples and cast to float32. Away from endpoint bins, the normalization gives an ensemble one-sided PSD $2B_k^2/N$ before cropping. Window leakage in a short crop further changes the frequency-dependent spectrum. $S_{\rm cal}$ is therefore estimated from independently reserved cropped noise examples on the actual evaluation grid. The Hann sensitivity check uses a periodic Hann window and the correspondingly calibrated windowed-noise PSD.

The empirical PSD includes the synthetic process and its out-of-band fill. Inverse-PSD weighting emphasizes the corresponding low-noise bins. Population statistics average each source mismatch equally and retain the complete generated population.

\subsection{Noise reference and training}
The noise reference is computed from three supplied arrays through $n_A=(n_Z-n_X)/\sqrt2$, using Welch segments of length 1024 and sampling interval 15 s. The arrays contain numerical values without detector-generation or unit metadata. Frequencies outside the reference range are filled with $10^{-40}$ in numerical PSD units. The normalization above defines the benchmark noise. An independent set of 200 cropped noise realizations determines $S_{\rm cal}$ for Sec.~\ref{sec:results}.

The single-channel mask-aware residual module uses 12,000 training waveforms and 1,500 validation signals, with six independent noise/mask views per training waveform. Its loss weights the mask-enhanced relative time error, relative complex spectral error and direct spectral mismatch by $1$, $1/2$ and $1/4$. Model selection uses a composite score with weight 0.75 for the full validation mean and 0.25 for the peak-masking mean. Validation selection retains epoch 1095 from a maximum of 1200. The test uses seeds 2026240000--2026240999. Figure~\ref{fig:loss} shows the validation history.
